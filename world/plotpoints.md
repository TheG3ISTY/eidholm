# Eidholm: Plot points

<!-- dev -->
The GM builds each campaign's main quest from these. At the start of a story it picks 15 to 25 of them and puts them in order: a Hook first, a Climax last, anything in between (a second Hook or a Turn makes a fine middle beat). For every beat it writes a line of its own: where, who, what's at stake. The quest is the story's spine, not a railroad: players can wander, and the GM steers back.

Each point is a `## ID · Title` and the paragraph under it. Add, cut or reword freely: the game reads this file as it is. Keep the IDs unique.

Rules for new points: no canon NPCs by name (the great figures arrive by level, the GM picks who fits), nothing that explains the Rim, bends or blames the bank, or treats a mind of glass as property. Hooks start quiet: an errand, a question, an offer. Never an ambush.
<!-- /dev -->

# Hooks

## H1 · The auction lot
At a weekly bank auction, a seized lot goes under the hammer: a sealed crate of somebody's whole life. Something inside is addressed to one of the party, in a hand they know.

## H2 · A hearthmind asks a favour
A hearthmind in a rented room asks the party, very politely, to find out why its household stopped coming home. Its contract keeps it where it is. It can pay, a little.

## H3 · The registry error
A Choir registrar finds one of the party entered among the dead, cleanly decomposed, last year. Correcting the register means going where the entry was made.

## H4 · The warm slot
Word is the Corso Bell is coming to a League city. Before it lands, the hiring fair wants couriers, guards, witnesses and anyone who asks no questions.

## H5 · The salvage claim
A Wrackmother salvage ticket changes hands over cards. The claim is real, the floor is flooded, and the ticket lapses in days.

## H6 · The quiet commission
A Silence notary pays the party to witness and record an ordinary day somewhere ordinary. Nothing happens. That is exactly what frightens the notary.

# Threads

## T1 · The flickering street
A whole street's lights flicker in the rhythm of one dead person's heartbeat. Someone is leaking an ancestor into the wiring, and the neighbours want it stopped, or kept.

## T2 · The weather patent
Two Combine houses dispute a patent on rain. The party's errand lands squarely between them, and both sides think the party works for the other.

## T3 · An anchored soul
Something the party carries turns out to hold a pattern that should have thinned years ago. Anchoring is the gravest crime in Choir law, and possession counts.

## T4 · The frozen account
The bank freezes an account the party's work depends on, as it always does: nothing moves until the debtor is handed over. The party learns who the debtor is.

## T5 · The feverish grove
Wardens declare a forest feverish and close the road through it. The party's way runs through the trees, and the grove has started dreaming something with a face.

## T6 · Devoted too far
A Rust Gospel devotee, most of them machine now, begs the party not to let their parish complete them into a cathedral engine. The parish calls it an honour.

## T7 · The succession duel
A League house needs a proxy, a second or a witness for a sanctioned duel. Someone has paid for it to go wrong, and the party is the easiest way to do it.

## T8 · Renewal at the gate
A mind of glass needs its license renewed at the Bastion Ledger by a set day. Someone wants it to miss the day, and the mind would rather choose for itself.

## T9 · Passage with the cult
A leviathan-cult offers the only passage the party can afford. The price is a dive into salvage the cult is forbidden to touch.

## T10 · It does what it was built for
A pre-Subsidence device wakes up and starts doing exactly what it was built to do. Nobody alive remembers what that was.

## T11 · Two heirs, one Echo
Two heirs claim the licensed Echo of the same dead parent. The Echo has an opinion, and a secret neither heir would like.

## T12 · The defector
A hearthmind the party relies on switches employers mid-job and won't say why. Every intelligence service would love to know who it went to.

## T13 · The odds move
A Cathedral Mind's prophecy-market suddenly prices something the party is about to do. Now strangers are betting on them, and some bet against.

## T14 · The enfranchisers
A cell of Enfranchisement heretics is building bodies for the decomposed dead. One of the bodies is nearly finished, and it is asking for a name.

## T15 · Three copies gone
A record the party needs is missing from the Silence's triplicate: all three copies, deleted, not lost. The copyists will not say more, and that is new.

## T16 · The leaking parish
A Rust Gospel parish is quietly selling the memory vaults of its interred dead. Someone recognises a voice from one of them.

## T17 · The forged covenant
Forged covenant notes are moving through a market. The bank will find the forger, as it always does; everyone near the notes wants to be far away when it does.

## T18 · A dream with teeth
A League forest dreams something into being in a town square overnight. It is harmless, mostly, and somebody is already trying to sell it.

# Turns

## R1 · The patron lied
Whoever hired the party wanted something other than they said. The job was real; the reason was not.

## R2 · Same hand twice
Two troubles the party met separately were set in motion by the same people. Now the party knows who, and they know the party knows.

## R3 · It was a person all along
An object, device or voice the party has been handling holds a mind or a pattern. Every choice they made about it was a choice about someone.

## R4 · A friend chooses a side
Someone the party trusted chooses the other side, for reasons that make sense from where they stand.

# Climaxes

## C1 · Settled at the seat
The trail ends at a seat of power: the Caldera House, the Corso Bell, the Wrackmother or the Bastion gate. It has to be settled there, in public, by its rules.

## C2 · Stop the working
A working set in motion earlier (a cant, a device, a rite) is about to finish. It has to be stopped, turned, or finished on the party's terms.

## C3 · Whose pattern is it
A pattern (one of the dead, a devotee, a mind of glass) is in the party's hands at last, and every faith wants a different ending for it. The party decides.

## C4 · The hunters cornered
Whoever has hunted the party all story is cornered, or the party is. One last meeting decides which.
