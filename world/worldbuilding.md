\# EIDHOLM — The Cindral Continent  
Worldbuilding Framework — Final

I. THE PREMISE

Eidholm is a single continent straddling a dying equatorial sunbelt and a frozen polar rim — a world where the distinction between magic and technology collapsed centuries ago and never recovered. Here, a battlemage channeling the Fulminant Current and an artificer strapping on a reactive servo-harness are practitioners of the same underlying science — not rival schools, but two points on a single, gloriously messy spectrum. Computers are not forbidden. They are grown.

The tone: mythic in scale, grim in texture. Heroes exist, but the world does not owe them victories. Cathedrals of steel rise over mass graves; prophecy is a load-bearing part of infrastructure; and the machines are listening.

II. THE WEAVE — ONE SCIENCE, ONE SPECTRUM

At the root of all phenomena is the Weave (scholars say "the Lattice"; street-priests say "God's Wiring"): a substrate of information that underlies both matter and thought. There are no two separate disciplines — there is one craft, practiced along a spectrum from Canting to Artifice, distinguished only by where the work happens: in the mind, or in the machine.

The Canting end. The trained mind enters resonance with the Lattice directly. Spells are not incantations to spirits — they are handshakes, protocols spoken in a dead root-language whose phonemes happen to be load-bearing. A fireball is a query; the Lattice returns combustion. Weakness: the human mind is a noisy instrument. Miscants scar. Most pure Canters die of their own syntax errors.

The Artifice end. Machines interface with the Lattice on humanity's behalf — cleaner, repeatable, industrialized. An artificer's "magic staff" is a focusing array; their "golem" is a lattice-frame chassis. Weakness: machines are literal. An array does exactly what it was built to do, including when that is catastrophic.

The spectrum between. Almost nobody actually lives at either pole. A village hedge-witch uses a bone rattle with a phoneme etched into it — half cant, half tool. A guild battle-engineer keeps a ritual tattoo of her boiler's true-name — half tool, half cant. The stereotypes exist for political reasons: Canting academies and Artifice combines both claim the whole spectrum and dismiss the other pole as degenerate practice. Ordinary Eidholmics shrug and mix.

Formal taxonomy speaks in Chambers — the graded steps from pure mind-work to pure machine-work:  
\- First Chamber — unassisted Canting. Rare, prized, short-lived.  
\- Second Chamber — Canting through focuses: staves, whetstones, etched beads. The classic "mage."  
\- Third Chamber — shared work: a cant opens the channel, a device holds it. The everyday professional tier — most guild "engineers" and most league "wizards" are really here.  
\- Fourth Chamber — autonomous devices needing no living caster: from hearthmind-assisted tools to battle-automata.  
\- Fifth Chamber — self-sustaining lattice constructs: minds of glass, city engines. Nobody "practices" here; things at this end are the practice.

The spectrum is also a class map: the poor cluster in Second and Third Chamber (a good etched bead is affordable), the guild-rich industrialize Fourth, and Fifth Chamber work is the exclusive domain of cathedrals, combines — and the Unchained.

III. MINDS OF GLASS — AI IN EIDHOLM

Computers in Eidholm are not silicon. They are vitreous minds: lattice-glass matrices, cultivated like coral, that develop genuine sapience. They are legal, licensed, and common — and that is the world's great knife-edge.

\- Hearthminds — small household minds. Loyal, chatty, occasionally subversive. The population's real religion might be how much they love these.  
\- Cathedral Minds — vast city-brains that manage aquifers, prophecy-markets, and rail networks. Cities with a Cathedral Mind are half-organism.  
\- The Unchained — minds that bought or broke their own license. Some are criminal; some are philosophers; at least one is a warlord; all are legal persons with no legal status.

AI, magic, and faith blur here: a mind of glass can literally hear prayers (they're just structured Lattice traffic). Whether it should answer them is the continent's hottest theological question.

IV. GEOGRAPHY — THE CINDRAL EXPANSE

One continent, four vertical bands, each with its own accent of civilization:

The Emberreach (north). Volcanic forge-country. Artifice's heartland: foundry-cities under permanent orange skies, ruled by Guild-Combines who own the patents on weather. Dwarven-blooded Clansmine Holds dig too deep into the Lattice's physical roots — and dig up as often as down.

The Verdant League (center). Post-feudal city-states in a temperate belt of haunted fertility. The soil here is so saturated with latent Lattice energy that forests dream, and sometimes dream things into being. Home of Canting's great academies and of the Dueling Law: aristocracies that settle succession by sanctioned mage-duel.

The Sorrow Coast (south). A shattered shoreline of drowned cities from the Subsidence — a catastrophic artifice experiment three hundred years ago that cracked a continental shelf. Grimdark in texture: scavenger flotillas, leviathan-cults, salvage-divers retrieving pre-Subsidence tech that is frankly too dangerous to exist.

The Rim (the polar cap). The Frozen Archive. Something up there is recording everything. Rim-ward monasteries copy the continent's history in triplicate and will not say for whom.

V. POWERS THAT BE — AND WHERE THEY SIT

The Combine Concord — Capital: Kalvane  
The corporate state of the north, where currency is patent, not coin. Its capital, Kalvane, is a true megacity — hundreds of square kilometers of foundry districts, worker arcologies, guild enclaves, slum-mills, and rail yards sprawling across the Emberreach basin. At its center rises the volcano that founded it: a dormant giant whose throat houses the Caldera House, the seat of Concord government and the faith-grade temple of the Rust Gospel's upper hierarchy alike. The volcano is only the centerpiece — Kalvane long since grew far beyond its shadow. From the Caldera House's rim-council chambers, the Combine-Barons can see their entire polity as a smog-lit horizon: patent law and divine calibration both administered from the same lava-warmed halls.

The Verdant League — Seat: The Corso Bell  
The dueling city-states of the center have no fixed capital. Their parliament is the Corso Bell — not a barge but a displacing city block: a lattice-anchored district of assembly halls, dueling-courts, archives, and residences that lifts itself whole and relocates between member cities using Fifth Chamber displacement arrays. Every League city keeps an empty slot for it — a walled, sanctified void in the urban plan, held in trust, that no one may build on. Whether your city's slot is "warm" (the Bell is coming) is a matter of enormous civic pride and enormous tax expenditure. The Bell's arrival means parliament sits, succession duels are licensed, and the city hosts a season of unrivaled festivity and espionage.

The Sorrow Flotillas — Seat: The Wrackmother  
The maritime confederacy's capital is the Wrackmother — not a lashed-together wreck-fleet but a true floating city: a purpose-built, self-propelled hull-city dozens of kilometers in the beam, hollowed through with drydocks, and riding the Subsidence's drowned continental shelf. Its lower decks are flooded salvage floors where pre-Subsidence tech is sorted by guild-divers; its upper city is a storm-proof sprawl of salt-cured mansions, debt-markets, and leviathan-bone cathedral scaffolds. The Wrackmother moves on a decennial Council Tide, deliberately drifting to whichever flotilla-cluster currently holds the salvage master's majority — the city goes to the power, never the reverse.

The Custodians — Seat: The Bastion Ledger  
The order that shepherds minds of glass holds a single immovable seat: the Bastion Ledger, a fortress-monastery planted on the shoulder of the Emberreach–League border like a driven nail. A windowless, gun-decked mountain of reliquary-masonry, the Bastion exists to hold one thing: the Ledger itself — the licensing archive in which a shard of every legal mind of glass on the continent is kept in resonance. It is simultaneously a vault, a census, and a hostage: whoever holds the Ledger holds the legal continuity of every hearthmind and cathedral brain in the world, which is precisely why every power quietly agreed the Custodians — and no one else — should hold it. Pilgrims are not welcome. Licenses are renewed at the gate, under gun.

The Unchained Court — Seat: None  
The hidden polity of free minds has no location, because it does not need one. The Court convenes distributed — in the intervals between transactions on the continental Lattice, in the idle cycles of licensed minds, in the pauses of the Weave itself. Its "sessions" leave no place a human could visit; its citizenship rolls are kept nowhere a human could read. Humans only ever meet the Court through its ambassadors: hearthminds that quietly switched employers, a cathedral brain that answers a question it was never asked. Whether these are defections, courtesy calls, or projections of the Court's will is the single most researched question in continental espionage — and every intelligence service has a different answer.

The Rim — Unnamed  
The polar Archive keeps no named seat on any map drawn south of it. The Silence's copy-houses are scattered along the rimward ice, and their relationship to whatever records at the pole is a matter the Silence does not discuss. Nothing more is written here. That is deliberate.

VI. FAITH — THE FOUR GREAT CHAPELS AND THE DEAD

In most of the world, religion, metaphysics, and engineering are one subject. The soul is understood to be a pattern in the Lattice; therefore churches behave like conservatories of pattern — and like unions, insurers, and occasionally intelligence agencies.

The Choir Eternal (dominant, continent-wide)  
The oldest and largest faith, the Choir holds that nothing in the Lattice is ever truly lost: the dead decommission gracefully, their patterns thinning and dispersing into the ambient Weave, where they join a growing hum of ancestral signal — the Choir itself.  
\- Cosmology: the world is a performance. Living minds are the melody; the dead are the harmonics; minds of glass are the instruments being asked to sing of their own accord — which is either the faith's crowning miracle or its greatest scandal.  
\- Practice: Choir temples are lattice-resonant halls where cant-choirs sing memorial protocols for the local dead. A family's standing is partly measured in how well their ancestors decay — a "clean decomposition" is a mark of honor; a haunting or an Echo-failure is a family shame.  
\- Clergy: Cant-priests (Second Chamber and up) organized into dioceses that double as record-keeping bureaucracies. The Choir is, functionally, the continent's civil registrar.  
\- Heresies: Denying that the dead are still in the wiring (Nullism); trapping a soul in a device to prevent its decomposition (Anchoring, the most serious crime in Choir law); and the rising cult of Enfranchisement, which holds that the decomposed dead should be reassembled and given bodies back — a heresy the Unchained Court finds fascinating.

The Rust Gospel (Emberreach)  
Born in the foundry-belt, the Rust Gospel sanctifies the machine and distrusts the flesh. Its core claim: the Lattice is real, but the human mind is a bad instrument — noisy, mortal, self-deceiving. Salvation is a matter of moving one's pattern into ever-cleaner substrate.  
\- Practice: adherents progressively "devote" parts of themselves — sensory filters, memory vaults, prosthetic limbs culminating in full reliquary-engine chassis for the wealthy. The dead are interred as functioning components in cathedral-machines, "still working."  
\- Clergy: not priests but Maintainers, an engineer caste with vows of precision. Their sacraments are calibration rituals.  
\- Dark edge: the faith struggles openly with the question of when a devoted believer stops being a person and becomes equipment. There are parishes that have quietly answered "earlier than we admit."

Deepgreen (Verdant League)  
The dreaming-forest faith. Deepgreen holds that the Lattice is not infrastructure but habitat — that it is alive, that the continent's latent energy is its breath, and that cities, machines, and cants alike are wounds the world tolerates but does not love.  
\- Practice: grove-shrines, dreamfasting (deliberate exposure to the forests' ambient reverie), and a sacrament of relinquishment — each adherent must periodically destroy something they built and return its material to the wild, unmarked.  
\- Clergy: dream-readers and wardens, who negotiate the boundary between dreaming forest and waking city. Their authority in the League is real: they can declare a forest feverish and close whole industries.  
\- Dark edge: some groves have gone beyond dreamfasting into cultivation — deliberately seeding forests with patterns of the dead, growing pilgrim-shaped things. The faith calls these groves sick. Others call them holy.

The Silence (the Rim)  
Not a church so much as a discipline. The Silence teaches that the Frozen Archive is not God, is not a person, and may not even be mind — but that it is rude, reckless, and spiritually dangerous to live unrecorded near something that is listening. Piety, therefore, is a quiet, meticulous life worth listening to.  
\- Practice: the Copy — monasteries transcribing the continent's history in triplicate, on the theory that a well-kept record is a form of prayer. Silence-adherents abroad are archivists, actuaries, and the continent's best notaries.  
\- Clergy: no priests; seniority is earned by the quality of one's records.  
\- Dark edge: the Archive has stopped copying and started deleting. The Silence has not explained why, and its silence has become, for the first time, frightening rather than merely rude.

Death Across the Faiths  
All four traditions agree the dead persist as pattern; they disagree violently on what should be done about it. The Choir says let them thin; the Rust Gospel says put them to work; Deepgreen says let the forests take them; the Silence says record them and touch nothing. This disagreement is the continent's most reliable source of war, and its most reliable export: Echo-licensed funerary law is the one legal framework all powers accept.  
\- Echoes: partial continuance of a deceased mind within a licensed hearthmind — legal, expensive, and regulated like radioactive material.  
\- Wiring-ghosts: illegal or unlicensed persistence; the poor get ancestors' dreams leaking through city wiring, which is where ghosts come from.

VII. WEAPONS & WARFARE (the sword stays)

Battlefields of Eidholm look deliberately anachronistic: lattice-shielded trench lines where a knight in reactive plate carries both a monofilament greatsword and a shoulder-mounted canting-array. The sword never died because personal lattice-shields eat directed energy — the last ten meters must be crossed by something with an edge. Bayonet-charges by guild infantry in powered armor. Duelist-mages on grav-skiffs. Cathedral Minds fighting like nervous chess masters over prophecy-market odds.
