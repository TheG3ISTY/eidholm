> *The story begins: Freya.*

**GM:**

A rented room on the edge of the Emberreach’s worker arcology smells of stale oil and warm metal. The single window is fogged from inside, the condensation tracing the shape of a hand that isn’t there anymore. A hearthmind sits on a low pallet, its glass-and-copper casing humming softly, its lensed eyes flicking between you and the door.

Freya stands framed in the doorway, her black oak staff resting against the jamb, the focus bead at her temple glowing faintly against the dim light. She’s been here before—she can feel it in the way the air thrums with half-remembered echoes, the kind that cling to places where people have walked out and never come back. The hearthmind’s contract is etched into the room’s resonance field: a simple agreement, a household bound to return, a debt paid in service. But the household is gone.

The hearthmind tilts its head, a gesture that feels almost human. "You were recommended," it says, voice layered like overlapping echoes. "Not by name. By the way you move through places that still remember. I need someone to find out why mine didn’t come back." It taps the side of its casing, and a small data-shard slides out from a seam. "Payment is negotiable. Information first."

Outside, the arcology’s corridors pulse with the quiet rhythm of shift changes, the distant clank of machinery, the murmur of workers heading home. A few streets over, the first flickers of the evening’s streetlights begin to stutter—an oddity, but not unheard of in this part of the Emberreach.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I tell the hearthmind a good mystery always captivates me, and I am willing to help, however before I can, I need to find an inn to rest for a day as I can feel my resonance pool is a bit low, and ask the hearthmind for a recommendation.

**GM:**

The hearthmind’s lensed eyes flicker once, twice, as if processing the request, then steady. It taps a rhythm on the pallet’s edge—short, short, long—and the room’s resonance field adjusts, a warm pulse of Canting threading through the air. "Rest is wise," it murmurs. "The Emberreach’s ambient Lattice here is thick with industrial noise; it’ll drain you faster than you know. There’s an inn two streets over, the *Ashen Hearth*—it’s licensed, quiet, and the innkeeper won’t ask why you need to recharge. Just say you were sent by the Hollowwood." Its casing hums, a sound like a held breath releasing. "I’ll wait. The mystery won’t vanish."

The street outside is already dimming as evening settles, the worker arcology’s glow shifting from orange to a deeper amber. The flicker in the streetlights is stronger now, pulsing in time with something deep below—like a heartbeat, or a machine breathing. A few workers pass by, their faces tired, their boots scuffing the cracked pavement. One pauses to adjust a flickering lantern, muttering about "ancestors’ echoes" before moving on.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I nod and make my way to the Ashen Hearth.

**GM:**

You push open the creaking door of the Ashen Hearth and step into a low-ceilinged common room where the air is thick with the scent of roasted lard and burnt resin. The inn is half-full with shift-workers and a handful of off-duty guild engineers, their guild sigils still faintly glowing on their collars. The innkeeper, a broad-shouldered woman with a face like weathered oak and a vest that shows off a network of old lattice-scars, looks up from wiping a mug with a rag that’s seen better days. She nods at the mention of the Hollowwood, and her eyes flick to your focus bead with a flicker of recognition—not awe, but something like respect. "Room’s three bonds for the night, or twelve if you want the back one with a proper canting socket," she says, voice rough but even. "No questions asked, long as you don’t wake the house with some grand cant. We’ve had enough of those lately." She slides a chipped ceramic token across the counter—a key, or a claim, or both.

The room she points you toward is small, its walls lined with thin lattice-insulated panels that hum softly at the edges. A single lantern flickers on a narrow shelf, casting long shadows. The socket in the wall is real enough, a brass-plated indentation with a faint glow at its center. Outside, the streetlights flicker again, stronger this time, as if something is drawing power from them. A cough echoes from down the hall—someone’s breathing is uneven, strained. The inn feels like a place that’s holding its breath.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I rest for a day to recover my resonance pool, and after make my way to the common room and approach the barkeeper, asking for the price of a stew and a cider.

**GM:**

The innkeeper sets down a bowl of thick stew—bits of tuber, shreds of smoked meat, and a broth that smells of garlic and iron—and a frothing cider in a chipped tin cup. She leans on the counter, her arms crossed over her apron. "Stew’s two bonds. Cider’s one. You look like someone who’s been chasing ghosts through the wiring," she says, voice low. "Heard the flickering’s not just here. Whole block’s got it. Like something’s listening." She glances toward the door, where the flicker of the streetlights casts jagged shadows across the floor.

A sudden thump comes from upstairs—a heavy, deliberate footstep, then silence. The innkeeper’s eyes narrow, but she doesn’t look up. The stew is rich, the cider sharp, and the hum of the canting socket in your room feels just a little too steady, like it’s listening back.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I slice the barkeeper a 2 covenants and tell him the rest is for him to forget he served me before eating the stew and drinking the warm cider.

**GM:**

The innkeeper pockets the two bonds with a nod, her grip lingering just a second too long on the coins. She exhales through her nose, a sound like a held tool finally being set down. "I’ll forget your face," she says, voice flat. "But the debt’s on my side now. If you’re running from something, don’t come back here."

Outside, the streetlights flicker again—once, twice—then settle into a slow, uneven pulse. The sound of heavy boots descends the inn’s staircase, slow and deliberate, stopping at the landing just above the common room. A shadow stretches across the floorboards, long and thin, like a man standing just out of sight. Then a voice, smooth and layered with echoes, speaks from the darkness.

“You took something that wasn’t yours.”

The lantern on the shelf flickers, its light dimming for a heartbeat, as if something just walked through it.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I ignore the voice and head back towards the hearthminds quarters.

**GM:**

The inn’s door groans shut behind you, the sound of the voice still ringing in your ears like an echo that refuses to fade. The street is quieter now, the flicker of the streetlights reduced to a faint, uneven pulse that feels almost like a countdown. The air smells of damp metal and something older—coal dust, maybe, or the faintest trace of ozone.

The hearthmind’s room is still there, the door slightly ajar as you left it. The hum of its casing is steady, but its lensed eyes flicker rapidly as you step inside, as if it’s already sensing something is wrong. The data-shard you were given sits on the pallet, untouched. The room’s resonance field feels thinner, strained, like a taut wire about to snap.

Then the door behind you creaks open again. Not the innkeeper this time. The silhouette in the doorway is taller, its edges blurred by the flickering light. The voice comes again, smooth and layered, but this time it’s not just in your ears—it’s in the wiring. The lights dim further, the shadows stretching unnaturally, and the hearthmind’s casing emits a sharp, dissonant note, like a struck glass. The air itself seems to thicken, pressing in around you.
