> *The story begins: Freya.*

**GM:**

The iron gates of **The Hearthside Bastion** groan shut behind Freya as the last of the morning fog curls away from the cobblestones. A city of layered bronze and iron, **Kalvane** is already awake: the hum of servitor-chimes, the clatter of carts on tram-plates, the scent of heated stone and spiced oil. Freya’s family’s hearthmind used to sit in the control hub of this ward, regulating the great boilers that keep the district from freezing at night. Now its license has been transferred to **Master Veyra**, a gaunt woman in a rust-colored coat who runs the Bastion’s **Fuel & Forge Office** with a ledger in one hand and a dataspike in the other.

Veyra spots Freya leaning against the gatepost and beckons her over. “You’re late,” she says, not unkindly. “The audit report’s due in two hours, and the Archive’s server block is throwing **404: Vanished** on half the entries from last night’s burn cycle. Someone’s scrubbed the logs.” She taps the dataspike against her palm. “You used to speak fluent Lattice to these things. I need you in the hub—**now**—before the Custodians’ collectors show up and fine us into scrap.”

A **Custodians’ black coach**, its brass sigils gleaming dully, is already turning the corner toward the Bastion’s main gate. The driver’s hand rests on the brake lever, but the horses are restless, sensing the heat of the forge and the tension in the air. Inside the hub, the great boilers pulse like sleeping titans, their pressure gauges flickering erratically. A junior tech—**Tarn**, a lanky boy with grease in his hair—stands frozen at the control dais, staring at the vanished logs on the main screen. “I didn’t touch it,” he stammers. “But look—**it’s not just the night’s burn**. Someone’s rewritten the *audit trail* for the last three days. The numbers don’t add up.”

The problem is simple: **someone doesn’t want the Bastion to know how much fuel it’s really burning**. And if the Custodians find out before Freya does, the fines will bury the ward—and everyone in it.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I inspect the system to see what I can find.

**GM:**

Tarn steps aside as Freya approaches the dais, his fingers twitching like he’s trying to remember a half-erased cant. The control hub is a chaos of brass levers, glowing runes, and a single cracked screen that flickers with **Archive 404: Vanished** errors. The air smells of overworked oil and ozone. Tarn points at the screen. “Here—three days of burn cycles, all **redacted** in the same way. But the *pressure logs* are still here.” He taps a secondary panel. “Look at the spikes. **Twice a day**, for exactly twelve minutes, the boilers drop to **zero load**—then spike back up like nothing happened. Like someone hit a kill-switch and turned it back on.”

A low, rhythmic *thrum* vibrates through the floor. The junior tech’s eyes dart to the doorway. “That’s the **external pump array**,” Tarn mutters. “It’s cycling now. Someone’s pulling power off-grid.”

Master Veyra’s dataspike chirps. She reads the alert, then looks up at Freya, her expression grim. “The collectors are five minutes out. Whatever you’re going to do, do it fast—and don’t break the boilers. The ward freezes tonight.” She tosses Freya a **pulse key**—a brass cylinder etched with Lattice sigils. “This’ll get you into the internal archives. But you’ll need to *ask* the system nicely. It doesn’t like strangers.”

**GM:**

The pulse key fits the archive slot with a soft *click*, and the internal system hums to life—but the interface spits back a **LOCKED: AUTHENTICATION FAILED** error. Tarn winces. “It’s asking for the hearthmind’s *true-name token*. The one tied to the license.” He glances at Freya. “Your family’s old hearthmind used to handle that. Before it got transferred.”

Freya’s fingers tighten around the key. The system isn’t just locked—it’s *rejecting* her. Someone’s rekeyed the archive to the new license holder’s signature, and without the hearthmind’s token—or its true-name—the logs are **gone**.

Outside, the Custodians’ coach rolls to a stop at the Bastion gates. A black-uniformed collector steps out, dataspike in hand, scanning the courtyard with cold precision. Master Veyra exhales sharply. “You’ve got until they cross that threshold. Figure out how to break in—or figure out *why* someone wants the Bastion blind.”

The external pump array thrums again, louder this time. The pressure gauges flicker wildly.
