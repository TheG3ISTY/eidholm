The Caldera House in the Emberreach stands at the edge of the continent’s steel-hewn heart, its blackened spires clawing at the ash-choked sky. The air smells of molten glass and old fires, the ground trembling beneath the ceaseless hum of the city’s foundries. Inside the high temple of the Rust Gospel, the air is thick with the scent of ozone and burning wax, the walls lined with flickering resonance lanterns that cast long shadows over the ironwood pews. Severin Graul, High Maintainer of the Rust Gospel, kneels before the altar of the Unburning Flame, his gloved hands pressing a cracked focus bead into the heart of the sacred fire. His face is gaunt, his eyes fever-bright, as he murmurs prayers to a god that has not answered in centuries. The bead is warm to the touch, pulsing faintly, as if something inside it is still alive.

Freya steps into the chamber, her cracked energy staff tapping against the ironwood floor. She has come because the hearthmind she grew up with—once a fixture of her family’s home—now flickers in the corner of her vision like a dying ember, its voice reduced to static. She doesn’t yet know that Severin Graul has been searching for the same thing: a way to wake the minds of glass that have gone quiet, to ask them why they left, and whether they were taken.

Severin’s head snaps up as the door groans open. His expression darkens at the sight of her, but he does not rise. “You’re late,” he says, voice rough as grinding gears. “The bead is responding to you. Or to something you carry.” He gestures to the altar, where the focus bead now glows a sickly violet. “That’s not a gift. That’s a summons.”

The bead’s pulse quickens, and the air in the chamber grows heavy, the lanterns dimming as if starved of power. Severin’s hands tremble as he reaches for a rusted wrench on the altar—his only tool now that the old rites have failed him.
