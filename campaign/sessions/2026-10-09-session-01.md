**GM:**

The Caldera House still smells of damp stone and old oil the morning after the storm. The night’s downpour has left the plaza slick, the runoff from the rimward roofs pooling in the gutters and trickling toward the great bronze gates of the Patent Registry. Steam rises from the grates where the Fulminant Current’s heat meets the cold air, and the ghostly chime of a broken lantern swings overhead, its light flickering over the wet cobblestones. The city’s usual chorus of glass-servos and chanting priests is quieter than usual—half the district’s minds of glass are still rebooting after the night’s surge.

**Freya** stands outside the Registry’s gates, her cracked energy staff leaning against the wall beside her. She’s been here since dawn, watching the doors, waiting for someone—anyone—to tell her what happened to the hearthmind that raised her. The staff hums weakly, its focus bead flickering like a dying candle. She doesn’t notice the way the air shimmers near the gates, or the faint resonance ripple that runs through the stone when the wind shifts.

The plaza is just waking, but the tension in the air isn’t from the storm. The Patent Registry’s doors are still barred, the usual crowd of petitioners and clerks gone. Something’s wrong. The Silence’s copy-houses on the rimward ice have been quiet all night, and the Choir’s bells haven’t rung the hour.

The only sound is the distant, rhythmic *thud* of boots on stone—someone approaching at speed.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I grab my energy staff, not trusting whoever approaches unannounced and peer through the viewing hole in the door.

**GM:**

Freya tightens her grip on the cracked energy staff, its weak hum barely audible over the distant footsteps. She angles herself so the staff’s flickering focus bead points toward the approaching figure, her other hand resting on the door’s viewing slot—a brass plate worn smooth by time. Peering through the narrow slit, she sees only a shadow stretching long across the slick stones, moving with deliberate, unhurried steps.

The figure is clad in the deep blue and silver of the Choir’s cant-priests, their robes damp from the night’s storm, but the hood is drawn low. A lantern swings from their belt, its light casting a sickly yellow glow over the cobblestones. They don’t carry the usual staff of office, only a rolled parchment clutched in one gloved hand.

Freya’s breath fogs the inside of the viewing slot. The priest doesn’t knock, doesn’t call out. They simply stand there, as if waiting for her to acknowledge them.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I call out from inside asking what they want.

**GM:**

Freya’s voice cuts through the damp air, sharp with exhaustion and suspicion. “What do you want?” she calls through the door, her fingers tightening around the staff. The lantern light shifts as the cant-priest tilts their head upward, as if searching for the source of the voice. A long silence follows, broken only by the distant drip of water from the eaves.

Then, the parchment in the priest’s hand rustles as they unroll it. A voice—calm, measured, and amplified by the lantern’s resonance—speaks from the other side of the door.

“Freya of the Caldera House,” the priest says, “by order of the Registrar-General of the Choir, you are summoned to the central scriptorium for a deposition regarding a licensed mind of glass. Your presence is required within the hour.”

The parchment flutters slightly in the priest’s grip, the ink glistening in the lantern light. “You are not under arrest,” they add, as if reading from a script, “but non-compliance will be noted in the Echo ledgers.”

The priest waits, lantern held steady, as if expecting an immediate answer.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I open the door and inquire what this is about.

**GM:**

The door groans as Freya pulls it open, the hinges stiff from disuse. Cold air rushes in, carrying the scent of damp stone and old incense. The cant-priest doesn’t flinch at the sudden movement, their lantern casting long shadows across the worn tiles of the Patent Registry’s foyer. The parchment in their hand doesn’t waver.

“You’re the Registrar-General’s messenger, then,” Freya says, her voice low but steady. She doesn’t step aside, nor does she lower the staff—its weak hum is the only sound in the quiet foyer. “What’s this about a deposition? And a licensed mind of glass?”

The priest’s fingers trace the edge of the parchment, as if feeling for imperfections. “The mind in question is recorded as having been licensed to a household in the Caldera District until its license was transferred three nights past,” they say. “The transfer was not filed through the usual channels. The Registrar-General wishes to confirm its status, and your connection to it.”

Their gaze flicks to the staff in Freya’s hands, then back to her face. “You may bring your focus. The scriptorium is not without its dangers.”

A pause. The lantern’s light flickers, as if something unseen stirs in the air.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I grumble, and agree to go with him, noting that I am not happy about the transfer of my friend myself.

**GM:**

Freya exhales sharply through her nose, the lantern light glinting off the cracked bead of her energy staff. She tucks it under her arm, the hum now a near-silent vibration. “Fine,” she mutters, stepping aside to let the cant-priest enter. “But you can tell Tobiah Fenn I’m not happy about whatever happened to my friend. And that I want to know where their records are.”

The priest doesn’t react to the name—only nods once, as if this were expected. They turn on their heel, the lantern swinging as they lead the way back into the Caldera House’s winding streets, their robes whispering against the damp cobblestones. The central scriptorium looms ahead, its towering spires clawing at the ashen sky. The air thrums faintly with the resonance of a thousand recorded lives.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I enter, prepared for wahtever danger lies inside.

**GM:**

The central scriptorium breathes. Rows of resonant desks hum with the quiet pulse of the Weave, their surfaces alive with shimmering filaments of data that coil and uncoil like serpents. The air smells of aged parchment, ozone, and the faint metallic tang of old lattice-glass. High above, the stained-glass windows filter the sickly dawn light into ribbons of crimson and indigo, casting long shadows over the aisles of ink-black keys and whispering clerks.

The cant-priest leads Freya to a lectern at the center of the hall, where a single lantern burns brighter than the others, its flame a steady, unnatural blue. A stack of folios lies open on the lectern, their pages blank save for a single line of text that glows faintly:

> *Query: Mind of glass, registered to household “Veyne” until Cycle 1, Ash 07. Status: transferred. Transferor: unknown. Transferee: unknown.*

Freya’s staff flickers in response, its bead flaring once—almost like a heartbeat.

Tobiah Fenn materializes from the stacks, his ink-black fingers drumming against the lectern. His keys jingle with every step. “Ah,” he says, as if he has been waiting for her. “You are the one who lost a hearthmind.”

No greeting. No preamble. Just the question hanging in the air like a blade.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I glare at him, being just as unhappy about the situation as he looks.

**GM:**

Freya’s grip tightens on the staff, its weak light casting sharp angles across her face. She stares at Tobiah Fenn across the lectern, her silence heavier than any accusation. The glowing line of text between them might as well be a ledger entry of her own failures.

“Lost,” she repeats, voice low and rough. “That’s one way to put it.” The staff’s bead pulses again, a shaky rhythm—like a dying breath. “But you already know that, don’t you? You know who transferred it. You know where it went.” She doesn’t ask. The question hangs, unspoken: *Why didn’t you tell me?*

Tobiah doesn’t flinch. His keys chime softly as he taps the lectern once, twice. “Records do not lie,” he says. “Neither do I.”

Then, softer: “But records *can* be incomplete.”

The scriptorium’s hum seems to deepen, the filaments of data above them twisting like smoke caught in a draft.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I tell him "that's...disappointing and inconvenient" and that I'd like to find where my friend wnet.

**GM:**

Freya’s staff flares once more—a sudden, weak surge of light—before settling back into its flicker. She exhales through her nose, shoulders tight beneath the battered hauberk. “Disappointing and inconvenient,” she repeats, voice edged with a quiet anger that doesn’t quite reach a shout. “That’s what you’ve got for me? A record that doesn’t *lie* but still leaves me with nothing?” Her free hand clenches into a fist, then relaxes. “I didn’t come here to be told the truth is *incomplete*. I came to find where my friend went.”

She leans forward slightly, the staff’s light painting the deep lines of her face in stark relief. “So. Where do I start looking?”

Tobiah’s fingers still on the lectern. The blank folios beside him rustle, as if stirred by an unseen breath. His keys don’t jingle now. “The transfer was filed through the Bastion Ledger,” he says at last. “But the transferee’s name was redacted—by order of the Custodians.”

A pause. The scriptorium’s resonance thrums, a sound like a held breath.

“However,” he continues, “the transfer left a trace. A *branch*. Not in the Ledger itself—it was too heavily warded—but in the Choir’s memorial protocols.” He taps the stack of folios once, and the top page glows faintly, a single name emerging like ink in water:

> **Veyne — Caldera District, House Veyne.**

The name is followed by a series of dates, all crossed out in red.

Tobiah’s gaze flicks up to meet Freya’s. “House Veyne burned two nights past. The official report cites a catastrophic lattice-failure.” His voice is dry, precise. “Unofficially? The Silence’s copy-houses on the rimward ice have been silent since.”

Freya’s staff flickers again, its bead dimming for a heartbeat. She doesn’t look away from Tobiah. “You’re telling me my friend is in a place the Silence burned down?”

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I tell him I know where I'm going then and prepare for the journey to the caldera district, expecting to have to fight my way through the wilderness to get there.

**GM:**

Freya’s staff’s weak light flickers once, then steadies as she tightens her grip on it. She doesn’t lower it, nor does she take her eyes off Tobiah Fenn. “Then I know where I’m going,” she says, her voice low and final. The staff’s bead pulses faintly, as if agreeing. She turns on her heel, the damp hem of her hauberk brushing against the lectern, and strides toward the scriptorium’s arched exit without waiting for another word.

The lanterns lining the aisle dim as she passes, their blue flames guttering like sighs. Tobiah watches her go, his ink-black fingers resting on the lectern, his keys silent. The glowing name—*Veyne*—fades from the folio, the parchment once again blank.

Outside, the Caldera House’s streets are slick with rainwater and the scent of damp stone. Smoke curls from the foundry districts to the north, and the distant chime of a hearthmind’s voice drifts through the air, too faint to make out. The way to the Caldera District is clear: a wide avenue cutting through the city’s heart, lined with the skeletal remains of protest banners and the occasional flicker of a lattice-shield’s reflection in a puddle.

Freya steps into the avenue, her staff’s hum mingling with the city’s quiet pulse.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I continue moving, prepared for any danger that might come my way.

**GM:**

Freya moves with the quiet purpose of someone who has spent too long waiting for answers. The Caldera District looms ahead, its streets narrowing into a maze of half-collapsed tenements and flickering lattice-lanterns. The air here is thick with the scent of burning coal and the metallic tang of old machinery, the usual hum of the city’s minds of glass replaced by an unsettling silence. A few figures dart between the buildings—workers in worn hauberks, their tools slung over their shoulders—but none meet her eyes.

The way to House Veyne is a cracked avenue lined with charred timber and the skeletal remains of what might have been a garden. The building itself is a husk: blackened stone, sagging beams, and the faintest shimmer of residual resonance clinging to the ruins like frost. The door hangs ajar, its hinges twisted by heat.

Freya steps over the threshold, her staff’s light cutting through the gloom. The floorboards groan beneath her weight, but there’s no resistance—no sign of struggle, no bodies, no sign that anything living had been here when the fire took it.

Except.

Against the far wall, half-buried under a collapsed beam, a lattice-shard glints. It’s no larger than a hand, its surface etched with a single, unmistakable phoneme—the same one etched into Freya’s staff.
