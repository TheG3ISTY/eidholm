**GM:**

The sky over the Caldera House is the colour of old brass, the rimward wind carrying a scent of hot metal and burnt stone. The Caldera’s great foundries belch smoke that drifts over the city, staining the spires of the Choir Eternal and the rusted domes of the Rust Gospel. Below, in the shadow of the Caldera’s outer wall, the Slag Ward steams and hisses—its walkways slick with runoff from the furnaces, its air thick with the clatter of hammers and the groan of overheated machinery. Here, the border between the living city and the dead machines is thinner than anywhere else in Eidholm.

Freya steps into the Ward from the southern gate, her battered hauberk creaking under the weight of her gear. The air is acrid, the heat from the foundries pressing against her skin like a living thing. She hasn’t slept in days; the dreams of the feverish grove still cling to her like a second skin. A flicker of movement catches her eye—a figure crouched beside a smoldering pile of scrap, their hands moving with practiced precision. It’s Ilse Marrow, Registrar of the Patent Rolls, her face streaked with soot and ink, muttering to herself as she jots notes into a water-stained ledger. She’s here because the foundries have been producing unregistered devices at an alarming rate, and someone—*or something*—has been filing counterfeit patents in the names of dead inventors. Ilse wants it stopped before the ledgers themselves become evidence of something far worse.

A hand clamps down on Freya’s shoulder. She turns to see a hulking figure in a heavy apron, his face lined with the scars of a lifetime spent in the forge. "You’re late," growls Severin Graul, High Maintainer of the Rust Gospel. His eyes flick to the Cracked energy staff in her hand, then back to her face. "The Choir’s been asking after you. They say you were seen in the Hollowwood. That’s *their* territory." His voice is low, urgent. "There’s a device in the lower foundries—one that shouldn’t exist. It’s not in any ledger. It’s not even *possible*." He leans in closer, his breath hot against her ear. "Someone’s digging into the Rust Gospel’s secrets. And I think they’re doing it with *your* old Warden’s help."

The foundries groan around them, the heat pressing in as the machines pulse with unseen energy. The problem is clear: a rogue device, unregistered patents, and a traitor—or a ghost—from Freya’s past. The question is whether they can reach the lower foundries before the device does something irreversible, or before the Choir’s inquisitors find them first.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I press him further for what else he knows.

**GM:**

Severin’s jaw tightens at the question, his fingers twitching toward the undevoted little finger on his left hand. The noise of the foundry swells for a moment—a boiler’s hiss, the creak of a crane—as if the world itself is holding its breath. Then he exhales through his nose, slow and measured. "It’s a *listening array*," he says finally, voice barely cutting through the din. "Not just any listening array. It’s tuned to the Lattice’s deepest currents—the kind that shouldn’t be accessible from a foundry floor. Someone’s reverse-engineered a way to eavesdrop on the Cathedral Minds. And worse... it’s recording *what it hears*." His grip on Freya’s shoulder tightens. "The Choir’s already sent Cassia Dorn sniffing around the upper stacks. She’s got a tuning fork that can *feel* trapped souls—and if this thing’s picking up more than just machine noise... she’ll find it. And then she’ll find *you*." His eyes flick toward the distant spires of the Choir’s temples, their bells chiming the hour. "We’ve got an hour, maybe two, before she’s through the ledgers and on our trail. The lower foundries are already locked down. The only way in is through the Black Tongs’ territory—and they don’t answer to the Rust Gospel."

His fingers flex, the undevoted little finger curling inward as if resisting the motion. "Three years ago, a team of Rust Gospel Maintainers—devout to the hilt—vanished while calibrating the inner sanctum. Their resonance signatures are still in the system. And this... this device is *broadcasting* every cycle it draws power. Someone’s listening."

A distant alarm begins to wail, its pitch cutting through the foundry’s cacophony. Severin’s head snaps up, his eyes narrowing. "Cassia Dorn. She’s not waiting for the ledgers. She’s already here." His hand shifts from Freya’s shoulder to her wrist, his grip iron. "There’s a service tunnel beneath the Black Tongs’ scrap-yard. It’ll take you to a maintenance hatch near the array. But the Tongs don’t trade in metal—they trade in *secrets*. To get in, you’ll need something they want more than coin." He presses a small, charred bead into Freya’s palm. "This is a *Glimmer* focus. It’ll let you weave a light that won’t draw attention. More importantly, it’ll let you *see* what’s down there before it sees you." The bead hums faintly, its resonance barely stirring the air. "The device is broadcasting in real time. Whatever it’s picking up, it’s already out there. And if the Choir gets to it first, Eidholm’s secrets won’t be secrets anymore."

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I pocket the bead Severin gave me and get ready to face the tunnels.

**GM:**

The foundry’s alarms rise and fall like a dying animal’s breath, the wail echoing off the smokestacks and the rusted hulls of idle machines. Severin’s grip on Freya’s wrist doesn’t loosen as they move, weaving through the press of workers and the hissing vents that bleed superheated steam into the air. The bead in her palm glows a sickly violet, its resonance pulsing in time with the distant alarm—a heartbeat of stolen power.

They reach the mouth of the service tunnel, its archway crusted with old slag and the graffiti of the Black Tongs: jagged sigils that look more like Lattice glyphs than spray paint. The tunnel beyond is narrow, the ceiling low enough that Freya has to duck, the walls slick with condensation that smells of oil and rust. The air is cooler here, but not by much—just enough to make the steam from her breath visible. Ahead, the tunnel splits: one branch slopes downward into darkness, the other climbing toward the distant sound of voices and the clatter of tools.

A figure steps out of the shadows ahead, blocking the downward path. She’s slight, her dark skin dusted with the same soot that stains the tunnel walls, her fingers stained with ink and oil. A Black Tong scout, her eyes sharp under a patched hood. In her hand, a monofilament knife glints dully in the bead’s light. "You’re not Tongs," she says, voice flat. "You’ve got Severin’s stink all over you." Her gaze flicks to the bead, then back to Freya’s face. "What’s in it for us?"

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I pocket the bead Severin gave me and get ready to face the tunnels.

**GM:**

The scout’s monofilament knife doesn’t waver, but her free hand drifts toward a pouch at her belt. Behind her, the tunnel’s downward slope is swallowed by darkness, the air heavy with the scent of damp metal and something older—something that hums just beneath hearing, like a tuning fork pressed to the world’s teeth.

From the darkness below comes the sound of movement: a slow, deliberate scrape of metal on stone, then a metallic *clink* as something heavy is set down. A voice, rough and amused, floats up the tunnel. "You brought Severin’s pet down here, Kessa? Bold. Or stupid." The scout—Kessa—doesn’t flinch, but her grip on the knife tightens. "I’m here for the *Eyes*," she says, not looking away from Freya. "The big one’s mine. The rest you can split with whoever’s dumb enough to follow."

A shape resolves itself in the tunnel’s gloom: a hulking figure, his frame built for strength not speed, his face half-hidden beneath a scavenged respirator. His arms are wrapped in overlapping plates of salvaged armor, and in his hands he holds a short, stubby device—its surface etched with the same jagged glyphs as the tunnel’s graffiti. The device’s lens glows a faint, sickly green, pulsing in time with the bead in Freya’s pocket. It’s pointed at her.

**Freya (TheG3ISTY):** I ready my stance to fight the scout off.

**GM:**

Kessa’s knife twists in her grip, the monofilament edge catching the bead’s violet glow as she shifts her weight—ready to slash not stab, her eyes locked on Freya’s throat. The scavenger tightens his grip on the device, his thumb hovering over a stud on its side, and the green lens flares brighter, its pulse syncing with the bead until the air between them seems to hum.

Then the tunnel shakes. A deep, resonant *thrum* vibrates through the stone, the kind that makes teeth ache and bones itch. Dust patters from the ceiling, and the bead in Freya’s palm flares white-hot for a single, blinding second—its resonance snapping into phase with the device. The scavenger’s lens distorts, the green light twisting into jagged static, and for just a heartbeat, the tunnel’s walls *breathe*.

Kessa’s knife slashes—but the blade passes through the air where Freya’s neck was a moment ago. Freya *moved*, the bead’s light flaring again as the tunnel’s resonance shifts, the vibrations now carrying the rhythm of a voice—not human, not machine, but something older. Something that *remembers*.

The scavenger’s device screeches, its lens cracking under the overload. The green light dies, replaced by a single, pulsing word scrawled in reverse across the lens’s cracked surface:

**“LISTEN.”**

**GM:**

The silence pulse from the device at Freya’s feet doesn’t just deafen—it *unmakes* sound. For a heartbeat, the tunnel is utterly still, the heat of the foundry muffled as if the world has been wrapped in cotton. Then the silence shatters.

Kessa’s knife flashes toward Freya’s ribs, her movement a blur of practiced precision. The scavenger with the chain-blade swings, the hooked blade hissing through the air where Freya stood a moment ago, the chain coiling like a living thing. The third scavenger—the one with the ruined listening device—staggers forward, his hands weaving through the air in a rapid, practiced pattern, his lips moving as he mutters something under his breath. The air around him *wrinkles*, the Lattice bending like disturbed water.

The bead in Freya’s pocket is gone—burned out, its resonance spent—but the tunnel’s walls hum with the same energy, the glyphs carved into the metal now glowing faintly, pulsing in time with the scavenger’s chant. The devices down the slope flicker in response, their lenses flashing in a rhythm that feels like a heartbeat—or a countdown.

It’s not for the living. And whatever’s behind it’s been awake since the Caldera’s first fire." But you’ll burn with it."
